### Who are you, and what do you do?

I'm [Melanie Walsh](https://melaniewalsh.org/ "Melanie's website."). I'm a writer and professor at the University of Washington. My research explores the intersection of books, culture, and data, looking at trends like BookTok, library checkouts, and AI-generated fiction and poetry. 

My writing has appeared in the [*LA Review of Books*](https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/beautifying-beale-street/ "Melanie's article about 'If Beale Street Could Talk'."), [*Public Books*](https://www.publicbooks.org/where-is-all-the-book-data/ "Melanie's article on finding book sales data."), [*Publisher's Weekly*](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/childrens/childrens-industry-news/article/98304-the-sneaky-gender-bias-in-picture-books-animal-characters.html "Melanine's article about gender bias in animal characters from children books."), [*The Pudding*](https://pudding.cool/2025/07/kids-books/ "Melanie's other article about gender bias in animal characters from children books."), and many other [academic venues](https://melaniewalsh.org/publications/ "Melanie's list of articles in publications."). I wrote an open-source [Python textbook](https://melaniewalsh.github.io/Intro-Cultural-Analytics/welcome.html "Melanie's Python textbook aimed at people in the humanities."), and I'm currently working on a book about American authors who went viral on the internet.

### What do you use to get the job done?

A crazy proportion of my job happens via laptop. Right now I use a 14-inch M3 [MacBook Pro][macbook-pro]. When I'm on campus, I hook it up to a nice [ultra-wide LG monitor][34wn80c-b]. Also on my desk: this UW-themed lava lamp (with Bluetooth speaker) that I picked up at a thrift store, and LaCroix cans. Next to my desk: a little electric heater that I feel fondly toward. 

For coding, I use [VS Code][visual-studio-code] and increasingly Claude Code. Perhaps misguidedly, I'm writing my book in a combination of [Markdown][], [Quarto][], and [Pandoc][], which lets me convert to any output format (Word document, PDF, website) or citation style on the fly.

Speaking of, I like my citations like I like my pizza: Chicago Style ([Lou Malnati's](https://www.loumalnatis.com/ "A pizza store in Chicago.") or bust). My go-to citation manager is [Zotero][]. I also use the excellent Zotero app on my 10th generation [iPad][], which I otherwise mostly use for notes when giving talks or presentations.

For collaboration, I use [Google Docs][google-docs], [Overleaf][], and [GitHub](https://github.com/melaniewalsh "Melanie's GitHub account."), as well as [Slack][] and [Discord][]. Miscellaneous research notes live in [Evernote][], while my [Notes][] app catches everything else—random work thoughts, to-do lists, groceries, bad poems, jokes that I think are funny.

I also make a lot of websites and data visualizations. I'm currently obsessed with Quarto, [Observable][], and [Svelte][] (I highly recommend *The Pudding*'s [Svelte starter template](https://github.com/the-pudding/svelte-starter "A starter template for using Svelte.")).

For the sneakerheads who care, I'm currently wearing Puma's Stewie 2: Water shoes when I go to "work" on the basketball court.

### What would be your dream setup?

Weirdly, I dream about writing at a desktop computer. There's something very appealing and romantic about this to me—I don't know why. Also, since staring out the window and daydreaming account for roughly 60% of my writing process, I fantasize about windows with exquisite views. 

My university actually has a lab on [San Juan Island](https://fhl.uw.edu/ "A research lab connected to the University of Washington."), and you can go there for writing retreats. It's probably the dreamiest work set-up I've ever had. Maybe they'll let me move in.
